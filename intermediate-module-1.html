Technical Indicators

Use momentum, trend, and volatility indicators to spot entry and exit signals with precision.

7 min read
Sources: CFA Institute, J. Welles Wilder
Last updated Mar 2026

Why Technical Indicators Matter

Technical indicators are tools that turn price and volume data into readable signals. Think of them like a dashboard in a car. The four most essential are RSI, MACD, Moving Averages, and Bollinger Bands. Each one watches a different part of the market: how fast prices are moving, which direction they're trending, how strong that trend is, and how volatile the stock has become.

Key Insight

No single indicator tells the whole story. Professional traders use confirmation: they wait for two or more indicators to agree before making a trade. This filters out false signals and keeps you from losing money on tricks.

RSI: Relative Strength Index

RSI is like a speedometer for stock momentum. It runs from 0 to 100. When it goes above 70, the stock has been rising fast—it might be due for a pullback (potential sell signal). When it drops below 30, the stock has been falling sharply—it might bounce back soon (potential buy signal).

Example: Tesla's RSI is 75 (overbought). The stock has rocketed up 15% in a week. RSI signals caution—don't chase the rally. Traders often look to take profits here.

How to Use RSI

  • RSI above 70 = overbought. The stock may be due for a cooldown or pullback.
  • RSI below 30 = oversold. Look for signs of a bounce or reversal.
  • Divergence: The price hits a new high, but RSI doesn't. This often signals a reversal is coming.

MACD: Moving Average Convergence Divergence

MACD is a trend-following indicator that watches for momentum shifts. It compares two averages of price: a faster one (12 days) and a slower one (26 days). When the faster average crosses above the slower one, it signals upward momentum. When it crosses below, it signals weakness. It's great for catching early moves in a new trend.

Example: Netflix has been stuck sideways for weeks. Suddenly the MACD line crosses above its signal line. Traders interpret this as the start of an uptrend and buy. Sure enough, the stock rises 8% over the next two weeks.

Components

  • MACD Line: The faster 12-day average minus the slower 26-day average. It shows momentum direction.
  • Signal Line: A 9-day average of the MACD line. Use this as a trigger: when MACD crosses above the signal line, it's bullish.
  • Histogram: The gap between MACD and its signal line. A growing histogram means momentum is strengthening.

Moving Averages: Golden Cross & Death Cross

A moving average smooths out daily price wiggles to show you the real trend. It's like squinting at a chart to see the big picture. A 50-day average shows the medium-term direction. A 200-day average shows the long-term direction. When the 50-day line crosses above the 200-day, it's a "golden cross" (strong buy signal). When it crosses below, it's a "death cross" (strong sell signal). These simple patterns have predicted big moves for decades.

Example: Apple's 50-day MA crosses above the 200-day MA (golden cross). This signals a transition from a downtrend to an uptrend. Many traders bought after this signal, and Apple went on to gain 20% in the following months.

SMA vs EMA

A Simple Moving Average (SMA) treats every day equally. An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) gives more weight to recent days. This makes EMA react faster to new price moves. For short-term trading, many prefer EMA. For long-term trends, SMA works fine.

Bollinger Bands: Volatility & Support/Resistance

Bollinger Bands are like guardrails around price movement. They're built from a 20-day average with an upper and lower band set 2 standard deviations away. Price bounces between these bands. When the bands squeeze tightly together (the stock isn't moving much), a breakout is often coming—a sudden big move in one direction. When the bands are wide apart (lots of volatility), the stock is active and emotional, and volatility may be cooling soon.

Example: Amazon's Bollinger Bands have been very narrow for two weeks—almost no price movement. Then earnings come out, and the bands suddenly expand. The stock jumps 12% in one day. The squeeze predicted this volatility burst.

Key Takeaways

  1. RSI shows momentum: above 70 is overbought, below 30 is oversold.
  2. MACD identifies trend changes via signal line crossovers; histogram shows strength.
  3. Moving averages (50-day and 200-day) define the primary trend direction.
  4. Bollinger Bands pinpoint volatility extremes; squeezes precede breakouts.
  5. Never trade on one indicator alone. Wait for confirmation from multiple sources.
