Types of Algorithmic Strategies

Momentum strategies ride trends: buy 52-week highs, short 52-week lows. Works in strong trends but reverses sharply. Mean-reversion shorts overbought (RSI > 70) and buys oversold (RSI < 30). Profits from short-term overreactions. Arbitrage captures price discrepancies (e.g., stock futures basis). Market-making profits from bid-ask spread.

Reality Check

Most algo strategies work great in backtests but fail live. Why? Costs (slippage 10-50bp), regime changes (2008 volatility spike breaks correlations), and crowding (everyone runs similar algos so returns decay). Sustainable edge requires unique data, skill, or speed others don't have.

Execution Algorithms: Minimizing Market Impact

TWAP (Time-Weighted Average Price) slices large orders into equal pieces across time. VWAP (Volume-Weighted Average Price) executes proportional to hourly volume. Iceberg orders hide true size to avoid spooking the market. Goal: reduce market impact and slippage.

Market Microstructure Basics

Order books show best bid/ask and depth. Bid-ask spread (typically 1-5 cents per share) is transaction cost. Latency (milliseconds) matters: if you're 1ms slower, your orders get front-run. High-frequency traders exploit this with colocated servers. Volume and volatility determine optimal trade sizing.

Building a Trading System Framework

Core components: data feed (real-time prices), signal generation (buy/sell logic), position sizing, risk management (max loss per trade), order execution, and performance tracking. Python, C++, or Java handle the engineering. Backtesting catches obvious mistakes; live trading reveals true edge.

Strategy Comparison: Pros & Cons

Strategy Signal Pros Cons
Momentum Trend following Works in strong markets Large drawdowns in reversals
Mean-Reversion Overbought/oversold Profitable in ranging markets Gets crushed in strong trends
Arbitrage Price discrepancies Market-neutral, low volatility Requires speed and capital
Market-Making Bid-ask spread Consistent small gains Inventory risk, adverse selection

Key Takeaways

  1. Algo strategies profit from trends (momentum), reversals (mean-reversion), pricing errors (arbitrage), or liquidity (market-making).
  2. TWAP and VWAP minimize market impact on large orders; iceberg orders hide size to reduce front-running risk.
  3. Market microstructure (bid-ask spread, latency, depth) determines actual execution costs and profitability.
  4. Successful trading systems require robust data, clear signal logic, tight risk controls, and realistic backtesting with slippage.
  5. Most retail algos fail because edge decays fast when everyone uses similar logic. Unique data or superior execution skill is required.
