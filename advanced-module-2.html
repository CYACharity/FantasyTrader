Linear Regression for Stock Prediction

Linear regression models relationships between variables. In finance, we regress stock returns against market returns: Return = α + β × Market_Return + ε. Beta measures market sensitivity. Alpha represents excess return unexplained by market risk. A stock with β=1.5 moves 50% more than the market.

Key Insight

Regression reveals correlations, not causation. A high R² (goodness of fit) doesn't guarantee future predictability. Markets adapt to known factors. Today's high-alpha factor becomes tomorrow's crowded trade with zero edge.

Fama-French Factor Models

The Fama-French 5-factor model explains returns using 5 systematic factors: market risk, size (small-cap premium), value (cheap stocks outperform), profitability, and investment (conservative beats aggressive). Decomposing returns into factors isolates true skill from luck.

Backtesting: Where Reality Checks Theory

Backtesting applies a strategy to historical data to measure past performance. Use in-sample data (training) and out-of-sample data (testing) to prevent overfitting. A strategy that works on 10 years of data but fails on year 11 was overfitted. Use walk-forward analysis: train on 2010-2015, test on 2016, repeat.

Common Backtesting Pitfalls

Survivorship bias ignores failed companies. Hindsight bias cherry-picks winning trades. Data-snooping fit curves to noise. Real slippage (market impact, commissions) often cuts profits by 50%. Backtests show gross returns; live trading deals with net returns.

Performance Metrics Beyond Returns

Sharpe Ratio = (Return - Risk-Free Rate) / Volatility. Higher is better. Sharpe > 1 is good; > 2 is excellent. Sortino Ratio penalizes only downside volatility. Information Ratio = Alpha / Tracking Error. Measures risk-adjusted alpha versus a benchmark.

Key Takeaways

  1. Regression quantifies relationships between variables. Beta shows market sensitivity; alpha is unexplained excess return.
  2. Fama-French factors decompose returns into market, size, value, profitability, and investment factors.
  3. Backtesting must use walk-forward analysis with out-of-sample testing to avoid overfitting.
  4. Slippage, commissions, and real-world execution reduce backtest profits by 30-70% in live trading.
  5. Sharpe Ratio and Sortino Ratio measure risk-adjusted returns; always pair with maximum drawdown.
