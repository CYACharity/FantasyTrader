Forex Fundamentals: Currency Pairs & Pips

Currency pairs trade with one currency against another: EUR/USD means Euros per Dollar. If EUR/USD = 1.0900, one Euro buys 1.09 USD. A pip is 0.0001 (smallest price move). A 1-pip move on 100,000 units = $10 profit/loss. Forex markets trade 24/5 with highest liquidity in EUR, GBP, JPY, and CHF pairs.

Critical Point

Forex leverage typically runs 20-100x. A 1% currency move becomes 20-100% account swing. Margin calls are merciless. Most retail forex traders fail because leverage and geopolitical shocks wipe out accounts.

International Diversification Benefits

Stocks in different countries have low correlation. U.S. equities correlate ~0.3-0.5 with emerging markets. Japanese equities correlation varies 0.2-0.6 depending on regime. When U.S. recession hits, international markets often outperform. A globally diversified portfolio uses 30-50% international allocation.

Interest Rates & Bond Market Dynamics

Bond prices move inversely to yields. When Fed raises rates 1%, existing 10-year bonds drop 8-12% in price. Duration measures rate sensitivity (duration = 8 means 1% rate move = 8% price change). Yield curve inversion (short rates > long rates) predicts recession within 6-12 months. Central banks control short rates; markets determine long rates.

Central Bank Policy & Market Impact

The Federal Reserve (U.S.), European Central Bank (ECB), and Bank of Japan (BOJ) set benchmark rates. Rate cuts boost stock valuations (lower discount rates). Quantitative easing (QE) floods markets with liquidity, raising asset prices. Tightening reduces liquidity and pressure valuations downward. Every FOMC press conference moves markets 1-3%.

Central Bank Region Policy Tool Market Impact
Federal Reserve (Fed) USA Funds rate, QE, balance sheet Global equities, USD, Treasury yields

Key Takeaways

  1. Forex pairs trade pips (0.0001) with 20-100x leverage, making it dangerous for retail traders.
  2. International equities provide diversification with 0.3-0.5 correlation to U.S. markets.
  3. Bond prices and yields move inversely. Rising rates hurt bond and equity valuations simultaneously.
  4. Central bank policy (rate decisions, QE) creates the broadest market themes that override individual stocks.
  5. Macro trends (recession signals, Fed pivots, geopolitical shocks) move entire asset classes 5-20%+.
