Special Situations: Spinoffs, Arbitrage & Forced Sellers

Long-Term Investing Advanced 3 min read

Hunting where others must sell

Most of the market is a fair fight against smart money. Special situations are the exception: corners where someone is transacting for reasons that have NOTHING to do with price — index rules, mandates, mechanics, panic. When the seller isn't thinking about value, the buyer doesn't need to be smarter, only present. This module catalogs the recurring machines: post-bankruptcy equities, index-exclusion selling, forced fund liquidations, odd-lot tenders, and the liquidation arbitrage of closed-end funds.

The unifying theory is Greenblatt's: returns hide where institutional constraints bind. A pension CAN'T hold a sub-$500M spinoff; an index fund MUST sell a stock leaving the S&P; a bond fund MUST dump equity it receives in a restructuring. "Must" and "can't" are the two most profitable words in markets.

The catalog, with mechanics

Post-reorg equities: companies exiting Chapter 11 hand new stock to former creditors — bond funds that are often forbidden or unwilling to hold equities. Their mechanical selling meets no natural buyer (no analyst coverage, ugly chart, tainted name), producing the market's most reliable pocket of mispricing. Index deletions: a stock dropped from a major index faces billions of forced selling on a known date; the empirical pattern is exaggerated decline into deletion and partial rebound after — the mirror of inclusion pops. Closed-end fund discounts: CEFs trade at market prices detached from their NAV; buying at a 15% discount when an activist is pushing for open-ending or liquidation converts the discount into return on a timetable. Odd-lot tender provisions: tenders sometimes accept ALL odd-lot holders (under 100 shares) without proration — a small, capped, genuinely free lunch that survives because it's too small for institutions to eat.

Odd-Lot Arithmetic

Company tenders at $54, stock at $51, odd-lots exempt from proration. Buy 99 shares: $5,049. Tender all: proceeds $5,346. Profit ~$297 (5.9%) in ~6 weeks, capped by design. Do this ten times a year across situations and a five-figure account earns institutional-grade returns on a strategy structurally closed to institutions. The catch: reading the offer documents is the entire job — "exempt from proration" is a sentence on page 40.

Process over prediction

Special situations replace forecasting with reading: the edge is in SEC filings — the Form 10 (spinoffs), the S-4 (mergers), the plan of reorganization (bankruptcies), the tender offer terms. The checklist: What must each holder class do, and when? Where do management's incentives point (their new options strike AFTER the forced selling)? What's the hard catalyst and date? What's the downside if the situation just… sits? Position sizing follows the shape: capped-upside situations (tenders, arb) sized small and diversified across many; asymmetric ones (post-reorg equity, spinco) sized as normal positions with normal risk.

The Honest Warning

This is the most craft-intensive corner of investing: dozens of hours per situation, most of which end in "pass." The published returns (Greenblatt's 50%/yr decade) came from full-time obsession in a less crowded era. The realistic modern prize for a diligent part-timer is a few situations a year that add real, uncorrelated basis points — and an education in market mechanics that upgrades everything else you do. If that sounds like a hobby rather than a lottery, you're the intended audience.
