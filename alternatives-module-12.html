Building a Multi-Asset Portfolio

Alternative Investments Intermediate 3 min read

Collecting assets isn't diversifying

You now hold the pieces: real estate, REITs, gold, commodities, crypto, and the traditional core of stocks and bonds. The intermediate capstone is assembling them — and the first, hardest lesson is that owning many things is not the same as being diversified. Stocks, REITs, high-yield bonds, private equity and crypto are, under the hood, mostly the same bet: economic growth with leverage. In calm markets they wiggle differently; in 2008 and March 2020 they fell as one.

The crisis correlation problem: assets that look independent in calm markets converge under stress.

Real diversification means owning things that respond to different economic forces. Growth assets (stocks, REITs, credit, crypto) love expansion. Interest-rate assets (Treasuries) love recessions and rate cuts. Inflation assets (commodities, TIPS, gold) love exactly what kills the other two. Trend strategies (managed futures) love sustained moves in any direction. A portfolio earns the right to call itself diversified when at least three of those four boxes hold something meaningful.

Two honest footnotes. 2022 broke the stock/Treasury hedge — both fell double digits as inflation forced rates up, which is exactly why the inflation box (commodities were +16%) and the trend box (managed futures +20%) exist. And crypto's correlation to tech stocks rose as institutions adopted it: the diversification argument for Bitcoin is weaker today than its 2017 marketing, which is worth admitting out loud.

A build sequence that survives contact

Step 1 — core first. A global stock index and a bond allocation appropriate to your horizon; this is 70–90% of the machine and it is boring on purpose. Step 2 — name the job of every alternative before buying: income (core real estate, private credit), inflation defense (commodities, TIPS, gold), crisis behavior (managed futures, long Treasuries), or asymmetric upside (crypto, early-stage anything). No named job, no allocation. Step 3 — size by risk, not dollars. A 5% crypto sleeve at 70% volatility contributes as much risk as ~25% in stocks; treat it accordingly. Step 4 — write the rebalancing rule (annually, or at ±25% drift bands) and obey it mechanically. Rebalancing is the only mechanism that forces you to sell what spiked and buy what everyone hates — the entire benefit of diversification is captured in that uncomfortable trade.

A Sample 100k Build

Core: $60k global stocks, $20k bonds. Alternatives with named jobs: $8k REIT index (income + inflation-adjacent), $5k broad commodities (inflation), $4k gold (chaos), $3k crypto (asymmetry — sized so a 100% loss stings under 3%). Every sleeve has a job, a size ceiling, and a rebalance rule. Nothing here requires believing any forecast.

The Test Before Buying Anything New

Ask: which existing box does this belong to — and is that box already full? Most "exciting" products fail the test by being growth-box assets (private equity, high-yield, thematic ETFs, most crypto) pitched as diversifiers. The boxes don't care about the pitch.
