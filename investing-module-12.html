Economic Cycles & Sector Rotation

Long-Term Investing Intermediate 3 min read

The economy breathes

Expansion, peak, contraction, recovery — the business cycle has repeated a dozen times since WWII, averaging five to seven years, and each phase reliably favors different corners of the market. Early recovery: banks (steepening yield curves fatten lending margins), consumer discretionary, small caps — the beaten-down and economically sensitive. Mid expansion: technology and industrials, as confidence funds investment. Late cycle: energy and materials, as demand strains capacity and inflation stirs. Recession: staples, utilities, healthcare — people medicate, refrigerate and pay power bills in any economy.

The classic sector rotation map across one full cycle.

The market moves 6-12 months BEFORE the economy: stocks bottom mid-recession while headlines are apocalyptic (March 2009, March 2020) and often peak while data still looks fine. That lead time is why rotating on published economic data is systematically late — by the time the recession is official, recovery sectors have already run.

Signals worth respecting (and their failure rate)

The yield curve: when 10-year Treasury yields fall below 2-year yields (inversion), recession has followed within ~6-24 months for nearly every case in 60 years — though 2022's inversion took so long to "work" that many declared it broken. Credit spreads: junk-bond spreads over Treasuries blowing past ~5% signal genuine stress; spreads are often earlier and more honest than equity indexes. Leading indicators: new manufacturing orders, jobless claims trend, housing permits. None of these times the market precisely; collectively they tell you which THIRD of the cycle you're probably in — which is genuinely useful, and all you get.

The Humility File

An investor correctly foresaw the 2023 recession consensus… which never arrived; staying defensive cost 25% of upside. Another correctly predicted 2020's crash cause was unforecastable, rotated to staples in February by luck, then missed the fastest recovery ever by waiting for "confirmation." Even RIGHT macro calls routinely lose money on timing. This is the base case, not the exception.

Using cycles without becoming a forecaster

The evidence-based middle path: tilt, don't time. Keep the diversified core untouched; if you act on cycle reads at all, shade 5-10% toward favored sectors — never flip the whole book. Rebalance into recessions: your existing rule already buys cyclicals when they're hated; that's cycle investing without forecasts. Use cycle awareness defensively: late-cycle euphoria (2021's everything-rally) is when you enforce position limits and trim leverage, not when you invent new theses. And know the sectors' characters permanently: staples and healthcare are ballast, energy is an inflation hedge, banks are a rate bet, tech is a duration bet. Owning all of them on purpose — weighted to your risk, rebalanced mechanically — beats guessing which inning it is, because nobody reliably knows the inning until the game is over.

The One Reliable Rotation

The only rotation with a century of evidence: from euphoria to fear and back, harvested mechanically by rebalancing. Sector prediction is a bonus skill few possess; rebalancing is a discipline anyone can hold. Build on the second, dabble in the first.
