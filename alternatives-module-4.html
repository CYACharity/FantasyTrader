Gold & Precious Metals Basics

Alternative Investments Beginner 3 min read

Why people still buy rocks

Gold has been money, jewelry, and crisis insurance for five thousand years. It pays no dividend, earns no interest, and builds nothing, so why does every central bank on Earth hold it? Because gold is the asset that answers to no government and no company. Its value doesn't depend on anyone keeping a promise, which is exactly what people want when they stop trusting promises.

That gives gold a strange job in a portfolio: it usually does little for years, then shines precisely when everything else is on fire. In 2008, while stocks halved, gold rose. In the inflation of the 1970s, gold went up more than tenfold. Between crises, it mostly sits there, which is the point. Insurance is supposed to be boring until the day it isn't.

What actually moves the price

Three forces do most of the work. Real interest rates: when savings accounts and bonds pay well above inflation, holding a metal that pays nothing hurts, and gold weakens; when rates are below inflation, gold's "no yield" stops being a flaw. The dollar: gold is priced in dollars, so a weaker dollar usually lifts it. Fear: wars, banking scares, and inflation surprises send buyers to the oldest safe haven. Notice what's missing, gold's price has nothing to do with earnings or growth, because there are none. It is purely what the next person will pay.

The Insurance Frame

A $10,000 portfolio holds 5% in gold ($500). In a crash where stocks fall 40%, that $500 might rise to $600 while $9,500 of stocks falls to $5,700. Gold didn't save the portfolio, it gave you $600 of dry powder to buy stocks at the bottom, which is what insurance is for.

How to actually own it

Skip the pirate-chest fantasies. Gold ETFs (like GLD or IAU) hold bars in vaults and trade like stocks, the standard choice, costing roughly 0.25–0.40% a year. Physical coins from reputable dealers work for people who want zero counterparty risk, but you'll pay 3–8% over the metal's value buying and lose similar selling, plus storage. Miner stocks are NOT gold, they're businesses that dig for it, roughly 2–3× as volatile as the metal, with all the usual ways companies disappoint. And silver? It's part industrial metal, part precious metal, cheaper per coin, wilder per year.

Sizing It

The serious research argues over whether gold deserves 0% or 10% of a portfolio, nobody serious argues for 50%. If you hold it, hold it small, hold it cheap (ETF), and judge it over decades. Its job is to be different, not to be the star.
