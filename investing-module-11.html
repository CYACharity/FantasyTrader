Position Sizing & Rebalancing Discipline

Long-Term Investing Intermediate 3 min read

The decision nobody researches

Investors agonize over WHAT to buy and improvise HOW MUCH — exactly backwards, because sizing is the decision that determines survival. The brutal base rate: roughly 40% of all U.S. stocks since 1980 suffered a permanent 70%+ decline from peak (J.P. Morgan's "agony and ecstasy" data), while the market's entire return came from a small winning minority. Any single stock can go to zero; sizing exists so that none of them takes you with it.

Why position limits exist: individual stocks produce a fat-tailed mess, not a bell curve.

Working rules that scale: individual positions capped at 5% at cost (serious conviction earns 5%, not 25%); single sectors under ~25% even via funds (ask 1999's tech-heavy "diversified" investors); employer stock under 10% total exposure, because your paycheck is already a levered position in that company — Enron employees averaged 60% of their 401(k)s in Enron. The asymmetry that justifies all this: a 5% position that 10×es changes your year; a 5% position that zeros costs you one mediocre quarter. Sizing converts catastrophic risks into annoying ones while preserving the upside that matters.

Let winners run vs. concentration creep

Buy a 4% position that quadruples and you now own a 14% position — congratulations and beware. "Let winners run" and "trim to size" are both defensible; the professional compromise is a tolerance band: let positions drift to 1.5-2× target, then trim back — capturing momentum while capping single-name catastrophe. What's not defensible is deciding AFTER you're up 300% and in love. Nvidia holders faced this exact test repeatedly; so did Cisco holders in 2000, with the opposite ending. Write the band before the gain arrives.

The Band in Action

Target 4%, band 2×. Position runs to 8.2% → trim back to 4-5%, redeploy into laggards. If it zeros tomorrow, you lose ~4 points instead of 8+. If it keeps running, your remaining stake still compounds. You traded some best-case for a survivable worst-case — that's the entire job.

Rebalancing: the machine that buys low

Portfolio drift is silent: a 60/40 left alone through the 2010s drifted to roughly 80/20 — maximum equity risk arrived precisely at maximum valuations. Rebalancing — resetting to targets on a calendar (annually is fine) or at drift bands (±25% relative) — is the only mechanism that systematically sells euphoria and buys fear without requiring courage in the moment, because the rule already decided. March 2009: the rule bought stocks. March 2020: the rule bought stocks. Your feelings, both times, screamed otherwise. Studies put the "rebalancing bonus" at modest levels (tenths of a percent) — the real product is risk control and the removal of the worst decision-maker you know from the loop: you, mid-panic.

Write It Down

A sizing policy fits on an index card: max position at cost, max drifted weight, max sector, max employer stock, rebalance trigger, and what new cash buys (the most underweight sleeve). Every blowup story you've ever read violated the card it never wrote.
