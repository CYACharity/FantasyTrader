Understanding Stocks

Learn the fundamentals of stock market investing

What is a Stock?

When you buy a stock, you're purchasing a small piece of ownership in a real company. Think of it like buying a tiny slice of a business - if you own Apple stock, you actually own a small part of every Apple store, every iPhone, and every product they create.

Each share represents partial ownership in a company

💡

When you buy a stock, you become a shareholder - meaning you're entitled to a portion of the company's profits and have a say in major company decisions.

How Stock Prices Work

Stock prices change based on supply and demand. When more people want to buy a stock (demand) than sell it (supply), the price goes up. When more people want to sell than buy, the price goes down.

How supply and demand affects stock prices

Real World Example: Tesla (TSLA)

When Tesla announces better-than-expected car deliveries, more investors want to buy the stock, driving the price up. If they miss their delivery targets, some investors might sell, causing the price to fall.

Market Capitalization

Market capitalization (or "market cap") is the total value of a company. It's calculated by multiplying the stock price by the total number of shares.

Market Cap = Share Price × Total Shares

If a company has:

  • Share Price: $50
  • Total Shares: 1 million
  • Market Cap = $50 × 1,000,000 = $50 million
